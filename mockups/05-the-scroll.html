The letter · III
go, make, ready. — a letter on the hours
Nº 052 · Santa Fe

Where the hours are hiding at your gallery.

Set from a short correspondence · a gallery on Canyon Road · the owner answers, evenings

The provenance letters

3–4 hrs / week

Collectors ask the same forty questions in different words. Given your artist files and your past answers, the reply drafts itself in your voice — you read it, mend a sentence, send it. The judgment stays yours. The typing doesn't.

Words for the new work

2–3 hrs / week

The work arrives, the photographs are made, and the words wait for a quiet evening that doesn't come. From your photographs and a few spoken notes, a draft is waiting before you've locked the door.

What never gets written

1–2 hrs / week

You said it plainly. One working hour a week becomes the statements and the announcements, drafted from what actually happened in the room — nothing leaves without your hand on it.

6–9
hours a week, sitting in these three alone

And under the hours, the larger thing: what you did on this page — asking in your own words, and being met — is the first genuinely new way to use a computer in decades. It is like the internet, but better, and it is that early. The advantage, as before, goes to whoever moves first.

This letter is yours whether we ever speak. What you said here joins the record melted down, like old type — recast without your name.
The ask · IV

An hour of my attention, on your material. Fifty dollars. The money is what makes it honest.

Free hours are worth what they cost. Mine begins before we meet — I'll arrive with something already built from your own shop, and you keep whatever we make, along with your letter, printed and set properly. If the hour isn't worth it, say so, and the fifty comes back.
